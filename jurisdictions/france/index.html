Executive Summary

Construction law in France is the legal and operational framework governing planning, permitting, design, procurement, construction, completion and use of building and civil-engineering projects. It combines private project agreements with public-law controls on urban planning, building requirements, site safety, environmental matters and public procurement.

Municipalities play a central role in urban-planning authorisations. A building permit, permis de construire, is required for many new buildings and substantial works, while smaller works may be subject to a prior declaration, déclaration préalable. The local plan, plan local d’urbanisme or PLU, and other planning rules shape whether and how a project may proceed.

French construction law also has a distinctive liability and insurance architecture. The Civil Code contains the decennial liability framework for certain serious defects, and the project owner is generally required to arrange dommage-ouvrage insurance before the construction site opens for covered works. These legal features substantially affect project risk allocation, documentation and post-completion exposure.

Private projects may use standard forms such as AFNOR contract standards or bespoke agreements, while public works are commonly documented through the Code de la commande publique and public-contract administrative clauses, including CCAG-Travaux where applicable. Foreign participants should align French permit procedures, mandatory insurance, safety coordination, labour and tax arrangements, contract allocation and French-language documentation before mobilisation.

Object Definition

Construction law is the professional legal function concerned with establishing and managing the legal framework for a construction project. In France it includes urban-planning controls, municipal building-permit processes, construction contracts, statutory liability and insurance, safety coordination, procurement rules where a public buyer is involved, claims management and dispute resolution.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The function exists to make project responsibilities, permits, contractual risk allocation, insurance and compliance duties understandable and workable before disputes or regulatory failures arise. A sound construction-law framework enables parties to determine who must perform, under which requirements, on what timetable and with what consequence if performance changes or fails.

PurposeTo organise construction activity within France’s urban-planning, contractual, liability, insurance, safety, procurement and environmental framework.
Primary OutcomeA legally coherent project structure: appropriate authorisations, documented responsibilities, workable contracts, relevant insurance, compliance records and a defined route for change, delay, payment and dispute issues.

Country Characteristics

French construction law is characterised by the interaction of municipal urban-planning control, detailed statutory construction rules, mandatory insurance and a long-established liability framework. The mairie is commonly the initial authority interface for urban-planning authorisations. The civil-law concept of réception, formal acceptance of the works, is a key project milestone because it has significant consequences for defects, liability and insurance.

Institutional StructureNational codes establish the legal framework, while municipalities administer many urban-planning authorisations and local planning rules.
Planning and Permit SystemThe local PLU and other planning rules inform whether a permit may be granted. Larger or significant works commonly require a permis de construire; lesser works may be subject to a déclaration préalable.
Liability and Insurance CultureDecennial liability and mandatory insurance considerations are core structural features of many building projects and must be assessed early.
Contract CulturePrivate contracts are negotiated or use recognised forms; public works commonly use the Code de la commande publique and may refer to CCAG-Travaux and project-specific documents.
Language ExpectationFrench is central to authority interaction, statutory interpretation, permits, insurance and binding project documents. English may be used commercially but does not replace French-language compliance materials.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

A project should be assessed as a sequence of linked legal decisions. The order varies by project, but late treatment of planning position, permit requirements, mandatory insurance, safety coordination, procurement rules or contract documents can create substantial execution risk.

1. Project and Site ReviewIdentify project scope, site, ownership, local PLU and planning conditions, environmental constraints and the anticipated authorisation route.
2. Planning and Permit MappingDetermine whether a permis de construire, declaration préalable, other urban-planning authorisation, environmental consent or specialist approval is required.
3. Insurance, Delivery and Procurement ModelIdentify applicable insurance obligations, select the delivery structure and assess public-procurement obligations where relevant.
4. Risk and Role AllocationDefine project owner, designer, contractor, site-management, SPS coordinator and documentation responsibilities.
5. Contract DocumentationPrepare or negotiate scope, drawings, specifications, programme, price, changes, payment, insurance, liability, reception, defects and dispute clauses.
6. Construction and ControlsMaintain compliance evidence, safety-coordination arrangements, inspections, change records, meeting records and notices.
7. Reception and Close-OutManage réception, handover, completion declarations, documentation, defects periods, final account and unresolved claims.
Decision sequence: Does the PLU permit the project? → Is a permis de construire or prior declaration required? → Which insurance obligations apply? → Is SPS coordination required? → Is the buyer public? → What evidence and notices must be retained through réception and post-completion?

Frequently Asked Questions

Is construction law in France only about construction contracts?No. It combines private contracts with urban planning, permits, technical requirements, mandatory insurance, statutory liability, SPS safety coordination, environmental rules and public procurement where applicable.
Who grants a building permit?A permit application is generally submitted to the mairie. The decision granting the permit is typically an order signed by the mayor, subject to the applicable legal framework.
What is a permis de construire?It is a building permit required for many new buildings and major works. Smaller projects can instead be subject to a prior declaration, depending on the project and location.
What is dommage-ouvrage insurance?It is insurance that the project owner, seller or the owner’s agent must obtain before site opening for covered construction works, under the French compulsory construction-insurance framework.
What is réception?Réception is the formal acceptance of the works by the project owner. It is a significant legal milestone for defects, liability, insurance and post-completion administration.
Can a foreign contractor work on a French project?Yes, but the participant must address applicable French permit, insurance, safety, labour, posting, tax, language and contractual requirements.
