Executive Summary

Construction law in Bulgaria is the legal and operational framework governing spatial planning, permitting, design, procurement, construction, completion and use of building and civil-engineering projects. It combines private project agreements with public-law controls on land use, detailed development plans, design visas, construction permits, technical compliance, site safety, environmental matters and public procurement.

The Spatial Development Act, SDA, is the central legal framework. A construction project may proceed only where permitted by that Act and the applicable planning documentation. The chief architect of the municipality is a central authority in the planning and building-permit process, including the issuance of design visas where required, approval of investment designs and issuance of construction permits.

The construction permit, разрешение за строеж, is issued at the request of the contracting party or person holding the applicable construction right. The permit file may require a current ownership or construction-right document, approved investment design, conformity assessment, utility connection arrangements, fire and public-safety input for relevant project categories and environmental assessment or another specialist administrative act where applicable.

Completion is structured through construction acts, technical and state acceptance processes. For construction categories I, II and III, the National Construction Supervision Directorate, DNSK, issues the permit for use following the prescribed acceptance documentation, including Act 16. Public works are subject to Bulgarian and EU-derived public-procurement rules. Foreign participants should align Bulgarian planning, permitting, technical, safety, labour and tax arrangements, contract allocation and Bulgarian-language documentation before mobilisation.

Object Definition

Construction law is the professional legal function concerned with establishing and managing the legal framework for a construction project. In Bulgaria it includes spatial-development controls, design and construction-permit procedures, procurement rules where a public buyer is involved, project contracts, technical conformity and supervision, site safety, completion and use permissions, claims management and dispute resolution.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The function exists to make project responsibilities, permits, contractual risk allocation and compliance duties understandable and workable before disputes or regulatory failures arise. A sound construction-law framework enables parties to determine who must perform, under which requirements, on what timetable and with what consequence if performance changes or fails.

PurposeTo organise construction activity within Bulgaria’s spatial-development, permitting, technical-conformity, supervision, safety, procurement and environmental framework.
Primary OutcomeA legally coherent project structure: appropriate planning basis and permits, documented responsibilities, workable contracts, technical and quality records and a defined route for change, delay, payment and dispute issues.

Construction-law work is normally activated by a defined project event rather than by a single abstract legal question. The work often begins before land acquisition, design-visa or permit preparation, a design appointment or tender issue and continues through acceptance, use-permit formalities, defects management and any claim process.

Country Characteristics

Bulgarian construction projects operate through a sequenced planning, design, authorisation, supervision and commissioning model. The applicable detailed development plan establishes the site’s development conditions. The chief architect has a central role in design and permitting, while the design documentation is subject to conformity assessment before it can support the construction-permit process in the applicable cases.

Institutional StructureNational construction law operates through municipal chief architects, local administrations, specialist public authorities, licensed consultants and state construction supervision.
Planning BasisThe detailed development plan establishes key land-use, building-line, height, density, intensity and development conditions for the relevant property.
Design and Permit SequenceA design visa may be required; the investment design is approved by the chief architect after applicable conformity assessment; the construction permit is then issued on a complete file.
Completion and UseConstruction categories I, II and III are put into operation on the basis of a permit for use issued by DNSK following the prescribed acceptance procedure and documentation, including Act 16.
Language ExpectationBulgarian is central to authority interaction, permit applications, technical documentation, statutory interpretation and binding project records. English may be used commercially but does not replace Bulgarian-language compliance materials.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

A project should be assessed as a sequence of linked legal decisions. The order varies by project, but late treatment of detailed-plan conditions, design-visa requirements, conformity assessment, permit prerequisites, safety duties, procurement rules or contract documents can create substantial execution risk.

1. Project and Site ReviewIdentify project scope, site, ownership or building right, detailed development plan, utilities, environmental constraints and anticipated authorisation route.
2. Planning and Design-Visa MappingDetermine applicable planning conditions and whether a design visa is required before the investment design is prepared or approved.
3. Investment Design and Conformity AssessmentPrepare the investment design and obtain the applicable conformity assessment, specialist opinions, utility arrangements, fire-safety input and environmental decisions.
4. Construction-Permit ProcedureSubmit the complete file to the competent chief architect for approval of the design and issuance of the construction permit.
5. Delivery and Procurement ModelSelect traditional, design-and-build, construction-management or other delivery structure; assess public-procurement obligations where relevant.
6. Construction, Supervision and ControlsEstablish contract, technical supervision, site-safety and quality arrangements; maintain construction acts, inspections, change records, meeting records and notices.
7. Acceptance, Use and Close-OutManage acceptance documentation, Act 15 and Act 16 procedures where applicable, permit for use, handover, final account, defects periods and unresolved claims.
Decision sequence: What does the detailed development plan permit? → Is a design visa required? → Which investment design, conformity assessment and specialist opinions are needed? → Can a construction permit be issued? → Which supervision and safety records apply? → Is a permit for use required before operation? → Is the buyer public?

Cross-Border Relevance

Foreign companies may participate in Bulgarian construction projects as developers, contractors, consultants, suppliers, investors or group entities. Cross-border projects require a clear connection between Bulgarian planning, permitting, technical-conformity and site-safety compliance and the contractual, corporate, tax, labour, posting, insurance and supply-chain arrangements used by project participants.

RecognitionEU-derived legal frameworks are relevant in several areas, but Bulgarian detailed planning, construction permits, technical conformity, supervision and domestic safety requirements remain central to work carried out in Bulgaria.
Foreign CompaniesForeign participants should assess establishment, registration, tax, labour, posting, insurance, site access, professional roles, technical supervision and contractual authority before mobilisation.
Language ConsiderationsBulgarian is central to authority interaction, design and permit documentation, statutory interpretation and binding project records. English may be commercially practical but does not replace Bulgarian-language requirements.
International RulesEU public-procurement rules, construction-product rules, environmental requirements, posting rules and worker-protection requirements may influence Bulgarian projects depending on the facts.
Typical RisksAssuming that a construction permit can be obtained without the required planning, design, conformity and specialist steps, or assuming that international contract documentation displaces mandatory Bulgarian technical, supervision or safety obligations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is construction law in Bulgaria only about construction contracts?No. It combines private contracts with spatial planning, design visas, construction permits, technical conformity, construction supervision, acceptance and use permits, occupational safety, environmental rules and public procurement where applicable.
What is the Spatial Development Act?The Spatial Development Act is the central Bulgarian framework governing spatial development, detailed development plans, design, construction permits, construction, supervision and commissioning.
Who issues a construction permit?The chief architect of the competent municipality generally issues the construction permit within municipal competence, based on the completed application and required design and conformity documentation.
What is a design visa?A design visa is an extract of the effective detailed development plan that identifies the property, surrounding development context and applicable building parameters and requirements where it is required.
What are Act 15 and Act 16?They are key completion-stage documents in the Bulgarian construction process. Act 15 records fitness for acceptance, while Act 16 forms part of the process for establishing usability and issuing a permit for use in the applicable project categories.
Can a foreign contractor work on a Bulgarian project?Yes, but the participant must address applicable Bulgarian planning, permit, technical, supervision, safety, tax, labour, posting, insurance and contractual requirements.
