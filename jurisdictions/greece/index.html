Executive Summary

Construction law in Greece is the legal and operational framework governing spatial planning, permitting, design, procurement, construction, completion and use of building and civil-engineering projects. It combines private project agreements with public-law controls on land use, building permits, technical requirements, seismic and fire-safety matters, site safety, environmental approvals and public procurement.

The building-permit system operates digitally through e-Adeies, the electronic permits platform of the Technical Chamber of Greece, TEE. The City Planning Agencies of municipalities, known as YDOM, are generally the competent bodies for issuing administrative acts relating to building works, unless special rules apply. Authorised engineers use the platform to issue and manage approvals, permits and revisions.

Law 4495/2017 provides a central legal framework for the control and protection of the built environment and establishes the categories of building works. A building permit is required for works that do not fall within the statutory category of small-scale construction works. The procedure is carried out online through the TEE electronic platform and is supported by technical studies, declarations and the project documentation required for the relevant building activity.

Greek projects may also require planning, environmental, archaeological, forestry, coastal or fire-safety review depending on their location and characteristics. Private construction projects are governed by the Civil Code and negotiated agreements. Public works are governed principally by Law 4412/2016, which transposes EU public-procurement directives. Foreign participants should align Greek permitting, technical and site-safety requirements, professional roles, labour and tax arrangements, contract allocation and Greek-language documentation before mobilisation.

Object Definition

Construction law is the professional legal function concerned with establishing and managing the legal framework for a construction project. In Greece it includes spatial-planning controls, building-permit procedures, procurement rules where a public buyer is involved, project contracts, technical and safety obligations, claims management and dispute resolution.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The function exists to make project responsibilities, permits, contractual risk allocation and compliance duties understandable and workable before disputes or regulatory failures arise. A sound construction-law framework enables parties to determine who must perform, under which requirements, on what timetable and with what consequence if performance changes or fails.

PurposeTo organise construction activity within Greece’s planning, building-permit, contractual, safety, procurement and environmental framework.
Primary OutcomeA legally coherent project structure: appropriate planning basis and permits, documented responsibilities, workable contracts, compliance records and a defined route for change, delay, payment and dispute issues.

Country Characteristics

Greek construction projects are characterised by electronic building-permit administration and the central role of authorised engineers. The e-Adeies system is used to issue and manage building-permit acts, revisions and related approvals, while municipal YDOM agencies are generally competent for the issuing function. The project must be classified under the applicable Law 4495/2017 category before the correct permit or small-scale works route is determined.

Institutional StructureNational legislation operates alongside municipal planning agencies, digital permit administration, specialist public authorities and local planning conditions.
Permit SystemBuilding permits are issued electronically through e-Adeies. A permit is required for works that do not fall within the legally defined categories of small-scale construction works or exemptions.
Professional RolesAuthorised engineers prepare, submit and manage the administrative acts and technical studies required for building works through the TEE system.
Specialist ConstraintsEnvironmental, forest, archaeological, coastal, fire-protection and island or protected-area constraints may add approvals, studies or conditions depending on the site.
Language ExpectationGreek is central to authority interaction, permit applications, technical studies, statutory interpretation and binding project documentation. English may be used commercially but does not replace Greek-language compliance materials.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

A project should be assessed as a sequence of linked legal decisions. The order varies by project, but late treatment of planning position, permit classification, technical studies, specialist approvals, safety duties, procurement rules or contract documents can create substantial execution risk.

1. Project and Site ReviewIdentify project scope, site, title, spatial-planning conditions, local building terms, environmental and heritage constraints and anticipated permit route.
2. Permit Classification and Planning MappingDetermine whether the works require a building permit, a small-scale construction works approval, another administrative act or a specialist approval.
3. Technical Studies and Specialist InputsPrepare the architectural, structural, energy, fire-safety and other technical studies required for the project and secure relevant environmental, heritage or authority inputs.
4. e-Adeies Submission and PermitAuthorised engineers submit and manage the applicable administrative acts through e-Adeies, with the municipal YDOM generally competent for issuance.
5. Delivery and Procurement ModelSelect traditional, design-and-build, construction-management or other delivery structure; assess public-procurement obligations where relevant.
6. Contract, Safety and Site ControlDefine contractual scope and risk; establish site-safety arrangements, technical compliance records, inspections, change administration and notices.
7. Completion and Close-OutManage completion, handover, final documentation, defects periods, final account and unresolved claims.
Decision sequence: What do the spatial-planning rules allow? → Is a building permit or small-scale works approval required? → Which technical studies and specialist approvals apply? → Can the permit be issued through e-Adeies? → Is the buyer public? → What records and notices must be retained through completion?

Cross-Border Relevance

Foreign companies may participate in Greek construction projects as developers, contractors, consultants, suppliers, investors or group entities. Cross-border projects require a clear connection between Greek planning, permitting, technical, heritage, safety and site-control compliance and the contractual, corporate, tax, labour, posting, insurance and supply-chain arrangements used by project participants.

RecognitionEU-derived legal frameworks are relevant in several areas, but Greek building permits, technical studies, local planning rules and domestic safety requirements remain central to work carried out in Greece.
Foreign CompaniesForeign participants should assess establishment, registration, tax, labour, posting, insurance, site access, authorised-engineer roles, permit procedure and contractual authority before mobilisation.
Language ConsiderationsGreek is central to authority interaction, e-Adeies permit files, technical documentation and statutory interpretation. English may be commercially practical but does not replace Greek-language requirements.
International RulesEU public-procurement rules, construction-product rules, environmental requirements, posting rules and worker-protection requirements may influence Greek projects depending on the facts.
Typical RisksAssuming that a building permit is the only relevant approval, overlooking environmental or heritage constraints, or assuming that an international contract template displaces mandatory Greek permit, technical or safety obligations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is construction law in Greece only about construction contracts?No. It combines private contracts with spatial planning, building permits, technical studies, safety, environmental and heritage requirements, public procurement and dispute resolution.
What is e-Adeies?e-Adeies is the electronic building-permit system operated through the Technical Chamber of Greece. Authorised engineers use it to issue and manage administrative acts for construction works, including approvals, permits and revisions.
Who issues a building permit?The City Planning Agencies of municipalities, YDOM, are generally the competent bodies for issuing administrative acts relating to building works, with the procedure carried out online through the TEE platform.
Is a building permit always required?A building permit is required for building works that do not fall within the statutory categories of small-scale construction works or other exclusions. The exact classification depends on Law 4495/2017 and the project facts.
What governs public construction procurement?Law 4412/2016 provides the public-procurement framework for public works, supply and service contracts and is adapted to the relevant EU procurement directives.
Can a foreign contractor work on a Greek project?Yes, but the participant must address applicable Greek planning, permit, technical, safety, tax, labour, posting, insurance and contractual requirements.
