Executive Summary

Construction law in China is the legal and operational framework governing land use, planning, project approval, construction permitting, design, procurement, construction, completion acceptance and use of building and infrastructure projects. It combines private project agreements with public-law controls on land, urban and rural planning, construction quality, work safety, environmental matters, bidding and government procurement.

The Construction Law of the People’s Republic of China establishes central rules for construction activities, including construction permits, contractor selection, technical documentation, project quality and site safety. Before a construction project begins, the project owner generally must obtain the applicable land-use approval, planning permit, construction permit and other approvals required by national and local rules, unless a statutory exception applies.

China has reformed its construction-project approval system around four broad phases: land use and planning permits, project permits, construction permits and completion acceptance. In practice, the exact sequence and authority interfaces vary by city, district, project type, industry and local implementation. The relevant municipal or county-level housing and urban-rural development authority is central to the construction-permit process, while project owners must also coordinate planning, land, environmental, fire and utility requirements.

Private projects are governed by the Civil Code, Construction Law, project documents and negotiated contracts. Public projects may be subject to the Government Procurement Law, the Bid Invitation and Bidding Law and related rules, depending on the project and funding source. Foreign participants should align local approvals, Chinese technical and safety requirements, contractor qualifications, tax, labour, data, contract and Chinese-language documentation requirements before mobilisation.

Object Definition

Construction law is the professional legal function concerned with establishing and managing the legal framework for a construction project. In China it includes land-use and planning controls, project approval and filing, construction permits, contractor qualification, procurement and bidding, project contracts, construction quality, work safety, completion acceptance, claims management and dispute resolution.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The function exists to make project responsibilities, permits, contractual risk allocation and compliance duties understandable and workable before disputes or regulatory failures arise. A sound construction-law framework enables parties to determine who must perform, under which requirements, on what timetable and with what consequence if performance changes or fails.

PurposeTo organise construction activity within China’s land-use, planning, construction-permit, quality, safety, procurement and environmental framework.
Primary OutcomeA legally coherent project structure: appropriate land, planning and construction approvals, documented responsibilities, workable contracts, quality and compliance records and a defined route for change, delay, payment and dispute issues.

The work often begins before land acquisition, planning approval, project filing, design appointment or tender issue and continues through construction, completion acceptance, operation and any claim process.

Country Characteristics

China’s construction-law framework is characterised by a formal sequence of administrative approvals and strong local implementation. National law defines the core construction, planning, safety and procurement frameworks, while provincial, municipal and county-level authorities administer many project approvals. A project’s city, district, industry, funding source and land status materially affect the applicable procedure.

Institutional StructureNational legislation operates through provincial, municipal, county and district authorities responsible for natural resources, planning, housing and urban-rural development, ecology and environment, emergency management, fire safety and public procurement.
Approval SequenceThe national reform model structures construction-project approval around land use and planning permits, project permits, construction permits and completion acceptance.
Construction PermitBefore commencement, the project owner generally applies to the competent construction administration authority at or above county level for a construction permit, subject to statutory exemptions and project conditions.
Local ImplementationThe practical process, online systems, forms, fees, review sequence and authority interface vary by location and project category. Local confirmation is essential.
Language ExpectationChinese is central to authority interaction, permits, approvals, technical documents, statutory interpretation, procurement and binding project records. English may be used commercially but does not replace Chinese-language compliance materials.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

A project should be assessed as a sequence of linked legal decisions. The order varies by city, project type and investment structure, but late treatment of land, planning, project approval, construction-permit, quality, safety, procurement or contract requirements can create substantial execution risk.

1. Project and Site ReviewIdentify project scope, location, land-use status, ownership or land-use rights, city-planning conditions, environmental constraints, investment structure and anticipated approval route.
2. Land Use and Planning PermitsDetermine applicable land-use approval, planning permit for land use, planning permit for construction project and detailed-control planning conditions.
3. Project Approval or FilingDetermine the applicable project approval, verification, record-filing or investment administration route and secure relevant project-level approvals.
4. Design, Qualification and Specialist InputsEngage qualified design, supervision and construction entities; prepare drawings and technical data; obtain environmental, fire, utility and other specialist approvals where required.
5. Construction PermitSubmit the completed application to the competent local housing and urban-rural development authority and obtain the construction permit before covered works commence.
6. Contract, Safety, Quality and Site ControlDefine contractual scope and risk; establish quality-management, safety, supervision, inspection, reporting, change, payment and project-record arrangements.
7. Completion Acceptance and Close-OutManage completion acceptance, relevant specialist completion processes, handover, final documentation, defects periods, final account and unresolved claims.
Decision sequence: Is the land-use position secure? → Which planning permits apply? → Is project approval or filing required? → Which qualified project entities and technical documents are required? → Can a construction permit be issued? → Which quality, safety and completion-acceptance records apply? → Is the project subject to public procurement or statutory bidding?

Cross-Border Relevance

Foreign companies may participate in Chinese construction projects as developers, contractors, consultants, suppliers, investors, funders, insurers or group entities. Cross-border projects require a clear connection between Chinese national and local planning, permit, quality and safety compliance and the contractual, corporate, tax, foreign-investment, labour, data, import and supply-chain arrangements used by project participants.

RecognitionInternational construction practices and project structures are common, but Chinese land-use, planning, construction permit, qualification, technical, safety and completion-acceptance requirements remain central to work carried out in China.
Foreign CompaniesForeign participants should assess foreign-investment structure, establishment, registration, tax, labour, immigration, contractor qualification, design and supervision eligibility, insurance, site access and contractual authority before mobilisation.
Language ConsiderationsChinese is central to authority interaction, permits, planning files, technical documentation, public procurement, statutory interpretation and binding project records. English may be commercially useful but does not replace Chinese-language requirements.
International RulesCross-border investment, trade, imported construction products, customs, technology transfer, data, sanctions, financing and international tendering requirements may affect Chinese projects in addition to domestic construction law.
Typical RisksAssuming that a foreign contract or technical standard substitutes for Chinese approvals, overlooking local implementation and qualified-entity requirements, or underestimating Chinese-language documentation, project approval and safety obligations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is construction law in China only about construction contracts?No. It combines private contracts with land use, urban and rural planning, project approval, construction permits, contractor qualification, quality, work safety, environmental rules, bidding, public procurement and dispute resolution.
Is a construction permit generally required?Yes. Before a construction project is started, the project owner generally applies for a construction permit to the competent construction administration authority at or above county level, subject to statutory exemptions and project-specific rules.
What is required before applying for a construction permit?The Construction Law identifies conditions including approved land use, a planning permit in urban planning zones, a selected construction enterprise, necessary drawings and technical data, quality and safety measures and available construction funds, together with other legal requirements.
What is the national project approval reform model?It structures the approval process around four phases: land use and planning permits, project permits, construction permits and completion acceptance. Local implementation remains project and location specific.
What governs public construction procurement?The Government Procurement Law governs covered government procurement using fiscal funds, while the Bid Invitation and Bidding Law applies to specified large infrastructure, public-utility, state-funded and internationally financed construction projects.
Can a foreign contractor work on a Chinese project?Yes, but the participant must address applicable Chinese foreign-investment, establishment, qualification, planning, permit, technical, safety, tax, labour, immigration, import, data, insurance and contractual requirements.
